Bodleian Libraries’ AI Policy
29 September 2026

    1. INTRODUCTION

The Bodleian Libraries’ mission is to provide an excellent service to support the learning,
teaching, and research objectives of the University of Oxford, and to develop and maintain
access to Oxford’s unique collections for the benefit of scholarship and society. In recent
years, artificial intelligence (AI) 1 has been changing how information is created, discovered,
                                      0F0F0F




accessed and used. This is creating both opportunities and challenges for libraries.

We recognise that AI can support better discovery of collections, improve access to
information, enhance services and assist staff in appropriate ways. Because of this, we will
engage constructively with AI and explore its potential where it can advance the Libraries’
mission. At the same time, our use of AI must protect trust in the Libraries, in the integrity
and provenance of our collections, in the rights and interests of creators and users, and in
our scholarly and professional judgement.

This policy sets out the principles that guide the Bodleian Libraries’ approach to AI. Taken
together, these 8 principles are intended to preserve and strengthen trust in the Bodleian
Libraries as a steward of collections, a provider of reliable information and services, and a
responsible user of AI.

The policy is intended to be read alongside relevant University policies and guidance and will
be supported by practical resources and use cases. It was shaped through an extensive
consultation process within the University of Oxford community and will be reviewed
annually in response to our community of users, and as AI tools and technologies develop.

    2. PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to guide the Libraries’ use, development and stewardship of AI
in relation to our collections, services and ways of working. It provides a shared framework
for decision making by the Libraries and a reference point for people who use, contribute to,
access or work with the Libraries and their services.

The policy is concerned with matters specific to the Libraries, so it complements, rather than
replaces, relevant University, departmental and faculty policies and guidance. If there are
more detailed rules or procedures that apply to your activities, you should follow those
requirements.

The policy sets out principles rather than an implementation plan or detailed procedures.
Guidance and procedures to help implement this policy are not set out in this document.
Everyone working at the Libraries has a responsibility to consider how these principles apply
to their work and how they may need to adapt their practices to put these principles into
effect.

       4. PRINCIPLES

      I. Trustworthy: Protect and enhance our collections

As custodians of information and cultural heritage, when considering the use of AI we will
always prioritise the integrity, provenance and long-term value of our collections and the
metadata describing them.

We will use AI in ways that support access to, and discovery of, our diverse collections. We
recognise that AI can support valuable activities such as metadata creation and enrichment,
search, discovery, reference extraction and navigation. When we use AI in these activities,
we will apply appropriate human oversight and professional judgement and will seek to
maintain the reliability and verifiability of information.

We will provide clear information about the way we allow our collections and metadata to be
used. We recognise that these will vary according to collection type, rights, licensing
arrangements and other relevant considerations. We will manage risks associated with
large-scale automated data extraction or AI-driven reuse.

We will consider how AI affects the visibility and representation of different forms of
knowledge. We will seek to avoid reinforcing existing biases or making collections,
languages, perspectives or communities that are less visible harder to discover.

      II. Open: Support responsible openness

We are committed to openness and to supporting legitimate access, research and reuse of
scholarly outputs and cultural heritage. This includes a commitment to uphold academic
integrity and the reliability of the information that we hold. However, we recognise that AI-
driven access and automated extraction can create new ethical, technical, operational and
rights-related challenges.

We will continuously seek a proportionate balance between openness – supporting access
to high-quality research outputs – and the protection and expectations of authors, rights
holders, depositors and content creators. We will review this balance as AI technologies,
user behaviours, legal frameworks and long-term impacts develop.

We recognise that the purpose of academic repositories is to enable research to be shared
and reused with as few barriers as possible. In determining how, and under what conditions,
we make our collections openly available, we will ensure that this purpose is balanced
against the ethical, operational and technical challenges associated with AI-enabled web
crawling and AI-assisted search. Where appropriate, we may apply technical, contractual or
other measures to manage these risks while preserving legitimate research and educational
use of collections.

   III. Ethical: Advocate for lawful and ethical actions

We will always endeavour to act ethically and lawfully. We recognise that ethical and legal
norms in the creation and use of AI are evolving. This process of changing norms is likely to
continue for some time as AI technologies fundamentally change the information ecosystem.

We will use AI in accordance with applicable law and our contractual and licensing
responsibilities. This includes requirements relating to copyright, intellectual property, data
protection, privacy, confidentiality and other relevant information law.

Our priority is to provide equitable access to knowledge, research and cultural heritage. To
achieve this, we may need to balance our contractual responsibilities with statutory
exceptions, permitted uses and other legal rights.

Where appropriate, we will also advocate for public policy reform in support of research,
education and cultural heritage. This will involve engaging with lawmakers and related
stakeholders to develop new regulatory frameworks that reflect the value and the importance
of libraries and cultural heritage collections.

We will consider wider ethical questions that are relevant to the library, cultural heritage and
higher education sectors. These include:

   •   the provenance and responsible use of content and data
   •   the impact of AI on creators and researchers
   •   algorithmic bias and discrimination
   •   academic and scholarly integrity
   •   the ethical and reputational implications of working with AI suppliers and partners.

We will exercise appropriate caution where legal or ethical expectations are uncertain and
will acknowledge and be transparent about relevant limitations, risks and areas of
uncertainty.

   IV. Proportionate: Adopt AI responsibly and proportionately

We will use AI where it is an appropriate means of achieving a clear service or
organisational purpose. We will refrain from treating AI adoption as an objective in itself. We
will consider non-AI alternatives where they may better achieve the same aim. Where
appropriate, we will provide an option for users to opt out of AI use.

Before we adopt, develop or scale significant AI-enabled services, systems or workflows, we
will proportionately consider:

   •   their strategic fit (including benefits for users and staff)
   •   operational feasibility (including security)
   •   financial impact (including over-reliance)
   •   reputational risks (including legal and contractual factors)
   •   ethical implications (including environmental sustainability).

We will consider how dependent we are on external AI providers and the implications of
supplier or partner arrangements for data, service continuity, interoperability, governance
and accountability. We will undertake appropriate due diligence on significant AI-enabled
partnerships and suppliers.

Where AI is incorporated into services provided by the University or external suppliers, we
will seek clarity about relevant responsibilities by working within the governance and
assurance arrangements that apply to those services.

   V. Accountable: Maintain human accountability

Human accountability and meaningful oversight will remain central to the use of AI in the
Libraries. We will assign responsibility at an appropriate level for significant AI projects,
deployments and decisions. We recognise that we may share accountability across
individuals, teams, managers, partners and the University.

We will use proportionate human review and professional judgement where AI outputs may
affect collections, services, users, decisions or the reputation of the Libraries. The expert
skills and judgement of staff is essential to any use of AI and will always be applied and
developed so that we can prioritise maintaining accuracy, completeness and verifiability of
outputs.

Each user of the Libraries’ services is responsible for their own use of AI tools. Staff and
users are encouraged to report concerns about, challenges with and corrections to AI-
assisted outputs. Staff and Library users who are using AI should be able to understand
when they can override or correct its outputs.

   VI. Informed: Support informed and critical AI use

We will support staff in developing the knowledge, confidence and skills needed to use AI
and to assess its outputs responsibly in their work. This includes signposting relevant advice
and guidance, as well as opportunities for professional development and support.

We will help our users to understand where AI-enabled features are used in our services,
and the capabilities and limitations of these AI tools. We will communicate about AI in clear
and accessible language and will signpost relevant University, departmental or faculty
guidance where appropriate.

We recognise the importance of staff expertise and professional judgement as AI changes
our services and ways of working. When considering significant changes in the use of AI in
our organisational processes, we will use appropriate existing governance routes.

    VII. Transparent: Be transparent about AI use

We will clearly state when AI is used substantively in Bodleian Libraries tools, collections,
systems and workflows. We will provide information that helps people to understand the
purpose and relevant limitations of those uses. We will follow good practice for transparency
as the relevant standards develop.

Where AI materially affects our Libraries’ services or outputs, we will, where reasonably
practicable, explain whether AI is being used. This includes the role of human review,
relevant limitations of AI, and how concerns or errors can be reported. We will avoid
presenting AI-generated content as though it were solely human-generated or independently
verified when that would be misleading.

We will be clear about the presence of AI features in third-party resources and services
where we have sufficient information to do so, for example whether these features are
optional or integrated. However, we recognise that some features and information are
outside of the Libraries’ direct control. Where we do not have relevant information – for
example, about the training data used in large language models (LLMs) – we will be
transparent about what we do not know.

    VIII. Sustainable: Minimise AI’s environmental impact

We recognise that the development and use of AI technologies carry environmental impacts
and wider sustainability implications, including energy use, water use, hardware demand and
reliance on digital infrastructure. We will use AI in ways that are proportionate, responsible
and aligned with the University’s and Bodleian Libraries’ sustainability commitments.

We will consider the environmental impact when adopting, developing, procuring or scaling
AI-enabled services and tools 3. This will include:

We will help staff and users to understand the environmental implications of AI so they can
make informed choices about its use. We will ask partners and suppliers to provide
transparent information about the environmental performance of their products, including
relevant reporting on energy use, infrastructure, model choice and sustainability
commitments, where available. We will review evidence of environmental impact through the
relevant bodies as AI technologies develop.
