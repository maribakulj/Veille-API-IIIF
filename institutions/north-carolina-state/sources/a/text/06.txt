 Slow Looking
 April 23, 2018
 Share on Facebook 
 Share by email 
 By: Brian Dietz 
 The IIIF (International Image Interoperability Framework), growing out of work started in the early 2010s, has developed a set of shared technical specifications  to support the sharing and reuse of online digitized resources. The NCSU Libraries has been involved with the IIIF community since 2016 and is one of the IIIF Consortium 's Founding Members. We've implemented the IIIF APIs for interoperability with other applications and services. This means that we've been able to use browser applications like Universal Viewer , while allowing scholars to use the same materials in their own version of an application like Mirador . Having more interoperable content available on the web from different institutions means that scholars can now explore and annotate resources from institutions around the world. It also means lots of fun experiments have been developed reusing these resources. 
 An application we've recently learned about that demonstrates this kind of reusability is Slow Looking (here's a  nice write up on Medium about the process of "slow looking" and the app development ). Slow Looking systematizes the sort of in-depth, square by square viewing one might do when taking a really deep dive. Take a slow look at a perennial favorite of ours, from the plans for Dorton Arena .
 Pretty great, right? Now, let's say you want to experiment with some of our other images with this app. Here's what you need:
 The link to the Slow Looking app, including the image location parameter:
 http://slowlooking.cogapp.com/?image= 
 The path to an image on our IIIF image server:
 https://iiif.lib.ncsu.edu/iiif/mc00227-003-ff0073-000-001_0004/info.json 
 The entire URL should look like this:
 http://slowlooking.cogapp.com/?image=https://iiif.lib.ncsu.edu/iiif/mc00227-003-ff0073-000-001_0004/info.json 
 Replace " mc00227-003-ff0073-000-001_0004 " with the item identifier of your chosen image, found on the item's page. Copy that URL into the Slow Looking app , and enjoy. Let us know what you discover in looking slowly.
 Inspiration for slow looking (and other kinds) can be found on Rare and Unique Digital Collections, which provides access to thousands of  images, video, audio recordings , and  textual materials  documenting NC State history and other topics. For another great resource on university history, be sure to check out our  Historical State timelines .
 Jason Ronallo contributed significantly to this post. He has been involved with the IIIF Audio/Visual and Discovery Technical Specification groups.
