Exhibits and other programming 
 Past Programs
 The Blavatnik Archive collaborates with scholars and academic and cultural institutions to create public programs for a wide range of audiences. Travel exhibits featuring collection holdings that bring to light unique histories and themes are displayed at large and small museums around the world. Some exhibits are produced for audiences on university campuses, in coordination with lecture programs and other events. Others, relfecting our commitment to honoring World War II veterans and survivors, have been local exhibits and community receptions to celebrate local heroes.
 The Dreyfus Affair in Postcards: 
 Going Viral at the End of the 19th Century​
 ﻿In the summer of 2024, the Blavatnik Archive presented an exhibit on the famous Dreyfus Affair as seen through the lens of “Dreyfusard” and “anti-Dreyfusard” postcards from the late 19th and early 20th centuries.﻿
 In 1894, Alfred Dreyfus, a Jewish officer in the French army, was falsely accused and convicted of espionage. The ensuing political scandal divided the nation, bringing it nearly to the brink of civil war. Dreyfus’s supporters included such famous writers and artists as Émile Zola, Marcel Proust, and Claude Monet; among his opponents were Jules Verne and Edgar Degas. For both camps, the stakes of the Affair were exceptionally high, as it not only tested the impartiality and fairness of the French justice system, but also became a way to question and reevaluate fundamental social values, including the attitudes toward religious and ethnic minorities in French society.
 Aided by expert commentary from Maurice Samuels, author of  Alfred Dreyfus: The Man at the Center of the Affair  (2024), the exhibit highlighted the way that picture postcards, a new and immensely popular medium at the time, both documented the case and allowed Dreyfus’s supporters and opponents to share their “takes” through what could be considered an early form of social media.
 Academic advisor
 Maurice Samuels

 Soviet Jewish Anti-Fascist Committee: 
 Propaganda, Advocacy, and a Call for Unity, 1941-1948
 ﻿ In 2023, the Blavatnik Archive organized an exhibit at the Center for Jewish History on the complex and tragic story of the Soviet Jewish Anti-Fascist Committee and its chairman, the acclaimed Yiddish theater actor Solomon Mikhoels.
 The Soviet Jewish Anti-Fascist Committee was created as a tool of World War II propaganda, but despite its limited mandate, its members—prominent writers, artists, journalists, and actors—increasingly felt their responsibility as Jewish representatives. As they published materials and organized rallies to raise support for the Soviet war effort and the USSR more broadly, they also attempted to advocate for Soviet Jews on a number of domestic issues, from the rising antisemitism to the need for a nationwide Yiddish newspaper. Through photographs and other historical artifacts, the exhibit highlighted notable moments in the committee’s history, from its founding to its 1943 American mission to the eventual persecution of its members.

 Idealizing Zion: Visualizing the Jewish State
 In the spring of 2023, the Blavatnik Archive and the Center for Jewish History launched an exhibit in tandem with the symposium "Zionism and American Jews: Bringing Us Together and Pulling Us Apart."​
 ​
 The exhibit featured early 20th-century postcards produced in Palestine, Eastern Europe, and Western Europe. 
 These fascinating historical documents reveal how early Zionists imagined the nascent Jewish homeland in three specific areas: agriculture, military defense, and urban design. By showing how Zionists sought to root the new community in the land, protect it from external threats, and house its immigrant population in new cities, the postcards express some of the powerful ideals that drove the Jewish national movement.

 Road to Victory: Soviet Jews in World War II
 Travel exhibit 
 2020–ongoing 
 ​
 The Blavatnik Archive launched an interactive multimedia exhibition titled “Road to Victory: Soviet Jews in World War Two” at the Russian Centre for Science and Culture (CRSC) in Paris on January 29, 2020. This traveling exhibit, created in honor of the 75th anniversary of the victory over fascism, features materials not found elsewhere to date. It brings to life the experiences of those who made and witnessed the enormous sacrifices required to assure final victory. The presentation includes eight interactive displays in French, Russian, and English, and offers intimate, deeply moving details of the lives of Jewish soldiers during the war. The exhibit's travel schedule, which includes venues in Europe, Israel, and the United States, was interrupted by the Covid-19 pandemic and is planned to resume in early 2022.

 The Jewish Ghetto in Postcards: From Eastern Europe to the Lower East Side
 Travel exhibit​ 
 2016–2017 ​​
 ​
 Presented at the Museum at Eldridge Street (New York, NY) and the Yiddish Book Center (Amherst, MA), the exhibit explored the American Jewish immigrant experience of the early 1900s, with a spotlight on postcards of the Jewish Ghetto on New York’s Lower East Side, long an immigrant gateway (during the first decades of the 20th century, the term “ghetto” was understood as the place where Jews lived in New York City). Images of bustling streets with pushcarts and horse-drawn carriages, a pickle vendor, and surprisingly beautiful tenements with laundry lines suspended between buildings recall a long-gone era. The postcards were collected in albums, sent as mementos from travels, or—as indicated by a message scrawled on one of the images—mailed by Progressive-era teachers and workers who wanted to show the atmosphere of the neighborhood where they worked. The exhibit featured original postcards and 5 enlarged reproductions from the Blavatnik Archive’s Judaica Postcards collection.​

 Lives of the Great Patriotic War: The Untold Story of Jewish Soldiers in the Red Army during WWII
 Travel exhibit 
 2014–2015 
 ​
 This exhibit was produced for university campus audiences and featured compelling veteran stories and rich archival imagery, along with extensive context materials on 20th-century Jewish, Soviet, and World War II history. The exhibit was displayed at libraries and university museums at Millersville University, University of Virginia, University of Michigan, Harvard University, and University of Southern California, Los Angeles.

 "Local Heroes" Program
 Community events 
 ​2014–2019 
 ​
 Committed to honoring and celebrating our veterans, in 2014 the Blavatnik Archive produced a series of local exhibits and community receptions. Individual display panels, based on the materials collected during the Archive’s Veteran Testimony project, featured veteran biographies, photographs, and excerpts from interviews. Special receptions were held throughout the United States: in Boston, MA; Brooklyn, NY; Cincinnati, OH; West Hollywood, CA; and San Francisco, CA. Receptions were also held in Minsk, Belarus, and in the Russian cities of Moscow, Nizhnii Novgorod, St. Petersburg, and Yekaterinburg.
