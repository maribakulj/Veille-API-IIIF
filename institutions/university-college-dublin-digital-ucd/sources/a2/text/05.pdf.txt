Terms of Use: User Guide
This online user guide is intended to answer any questions you may have about re-using the content
found in the UCD Digital Library.

UCD Digital Library also has a full UCD Digital Library Rights, Access, and Re-use Policy which covers
areas such as copyright, GDPR, cookies and user statistics. It also includes the Take Down Policy.

If you have any queries about this user guide, or the full policy, please contact us at
digital.library@ucd.ie.
Re-use of UCD Digital Library Content
Introduction
UCD Digital Library publishes a wide variety of digital content from research centres and cultural
heritage repositories (libraries and archives) within UCD, as well as external partners. Our primary goal is
to facilitate the re-use of the hidden treasures found in physical repositories, and by making the digital
content freely available in the UCD Digital Library, we are enabling the re-use of this material for
personal and scholarly purposes. Commercial re-use has restrictions, which are detailed below.

Copyright
All of the digital content in the UCD Digital Library is assigned a copyright status, and there are three
main categories:
     1. Public domain/Out of copyright
     2. Copyright unknown, making the content an Orphan Work
     3. In copyright, where permission to re-use may need to be sought

The copyright status is declared in the digital item record as a Rights Statement in the Standardized
Rights field. If the item is in copyright, it may also have a Creative Commons license (CC license), which
details how it may be re-used.

Copyright is a legal status and should not be ignored. Copyright (or the right to make a copy) belongs to
the creator of the content and endures for a specified time after the person's death. UCD Digital Library
ensures, where possible, that the correct copyright status is applied to each digital item, and we manage
the copyright information in an ethical and GDPR-compliant way.

You can read our full UCD Digital Library Rights, Access, and Re-use Policy for more information on
definitions, licenses, and how we manage the copyright of our digital content.

Other conditions
The digital items available on the UCD Digital Library are an exact representation of the physical material
available in the source repositories. As such, the source repositories may put further conditions on their
re-use, separate to copyright.

The item record will state if such conditions exist, and we will endeavour to supply you with this
information, where known. We are bound by the deposit agreements, collection procedures and
acquisition policies of our depositors.

Sensitive material
Users are advised that UCD Digital Library may contain material or language that could cause distress or
offense, or material that would be considered culturally sensitive or inappropriate today. While UCD
Digital Library does not endorse such material, it is made available, in its original form and context, in
the interests of historical integrity, the representation of diverse views, and for research purposes. The
opinions expressed in the content of UCD Digital Library do not necessarily reflect those of the UCD
Digital Library or University College Dublin. Any material or language considered inappropriate that is
not indicated as such in the descriptive information, should be flagged with the UCD Digital Library by
email, at digital.library@ucd.ie.
FAQ
The following questions and answers should cover any queries that you may have in relation to re-using
the content in the UCD Digital Library. If you do not find an answer to your question, please contact us
at digital.library@ucd.ie

1 Re-use

Do I need to ask for permission to use an item?
This will depend on the copyright status of the item and on how you intend to use it. The information
UCD Digital Library has about the copyright status of items are detailed in either a Rights Statement or
Creative Commons License in the Rights and Standardized Rights fields. Please always check, as this is
your legal responsibility to investigate copyright for your use.

First, check the record for the item to see which Rights Statement or Creative Commons License it has. If
it has a CC0 License or Public Domain Mark, then you are free to use the item for any purpose, although
crediting us as the source would be nice and will help other people find our resources.

For all other licenses or rights statements, our guides to Creative Commons Licenses and Rights
Statements at the end of this User Guide explain how the items can be used and whether permission is
needed.

Second, you will need to decide if your usage is commercial or not - see our explanation below.

Is my use commercial or non-commercial?
UCD Digital Library considers that personal or scholarly use is generally non-commercial.

If there is a financial transaction involved, if you gain a commercial advantage, or you receive monetary
compensation, then it is likely to be considered commercial.

If you are unsure if your use is commercial or not, please contact us at digital.library@ucd.ie to discuss
it.

I want to use an item for a purpose that may be commercial but the Creative Commons License or
Rights Statement does not permit commercial use.
A Creative Commons license with an NC (non-commercial) clause means you cannot use the item for
commercial use under the terms of that license. This license has been assigned to the item by the
copyright holder, and is part of the conditions allowing publication in UCD Digital Library. You may,
however, be able to get permission for your commercial use, directly from the copyright holder.

Similarly, a Rights Statement may not allow an item to be used for a commercial use without permission
but you may be able to secure permission directly from the copyright holder.

See our question on who to contact for permissions.
The item I want to use is described as an “Orphan Work”- what does this mean?
An orphan work is an item that is possibly in copyright but the rights holders have either not been
identified or they cannot be located, despite our best efforts to contact them. UCD Digital Library is able
to publish these items online due to an EU Directive. Orphan works cannot be used for commercial
purposes unless you have the permission of the copyright holders. As UCD Digital Library has been
unable to trace the copyright holders, we may not be able to assist you in getting permission to use the
item.

The item I want to use is described as having mixed rights - what does this mean?
Some items are made up of a number of parts that have different creators and therefore a mixture of
different copyright statuses. For example, an item may be made up of a number of letters written by
different people. Where an item has a mix of copyright statuses, we will apply the Rights Statement or
Creative Commons licence for the most restrictive part to the entire item and add a Rights Note
explaining that the item has a mix of statuses. If you want to use a particular part or parts of such an
item, please contact us, and we will check the exact status of that part.

I need permission to use an item, who do I contact?
Please contact us to request permission, preferably by email: digital.library@ucd.ie.

We will need full details of which items you want to use - please supply the reference link and the name
of the collection to which the item belongs. We will also need the details of how you want to use the
item and the audience for your intended use.

We may be able to grant permission for some items but for other items your request may be sent onto
the source repository. Your request may take time to process, so please allow for this.

For other items, we may advise you to contact the copyright holder directly. In these circumstances, we
will endeavour to assist you in contacting them, within the constraints of GDPR.

What is the process for granting permission to re-use items?
You can use the digital content for personal and scholarly use, without permission.

However, some scholarly use can be seen as commercial activity, such as if there is a financial
transaction involved, if you gain a commercial advantage, or you receive monetary compensation. If you
are unsure if your use is commercial or not, please contact us at digital.library@ucd.ie to discuss it.

For commercial use, we liaise with the copyright holders and source repositories, and ultimately the
decision belongs to them.

2. Image Supply

How do I get a high quality (high resolution) version of an item?
The download images provided on our website should suit most uses. If, however, you need a higher
resolution than the website can provide, or you need an image in a different format, please contact us.
Can you print a high quality copy of the item for me?
Unfortunately, UCD Digital Library has no printing facilities. Please contact us and we can see if a high
quality image file can be supplied to you that you can then take to be printed professionally.

Do you charge for supplying high quality copies of the items?
UCD Digital Library does not have a policy of charging for digital copies; however, there may be an
administrative charge for some items in our collections. If you have requested one of these items, these
charges will be discussed with you.

How long will it take to receive high quality copies of the digital items?
There may be delays in the supply of the digital items, due to workload and staffing issues. Please allow
10 working days for the supply of the items.

I need an item that is not already in the UCD Digital Library. Can you digitise it for me?
Unfortunately, we are unable to offer a “digitisation on demand” service at this time due to limited
resources and current priorities for digitisation. If the items are held in UCD source repositories, you can
contact them directly and they may be able to supply you with a copy, within their own workload
constraints.

The item I need comes as two images. Can you stitch them together or re-scan as a single image?
Items in the UCD Digital Library may have been scanned under the constraints of the equipment of the
time, or due to the condition of the physical item. Unfortunately, due to limited resources and current
priorities for digitisation, we are unable to facilitate these requests. You can contact the source
repository directly about alternative options.

3. Metadata

Can I use your metadata (descriptions of items)?
Yes! We publish our metadata in the descriptive records under a Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal
Public Domain Dedication so it can be used free of any restrictions. We just ask that any use of our
descriptions is acknowledged and cited.

I have spotted an error in your metadata (descriptions), how can it be corrected?
We create our descriptions from information provided by the source repository or the creator of the
item, and from our own research. Our descriptions are (usually!) correct at the time of cataloguing. If
you spot an error, please do let us know at digital.library@ucd.ie.

Due to the many procedures and systems that contain our metadata, it might be some time before any
changes are represented in the UCD Digital Library.

How do I cite the material?
Each of our items has a preferred citation as part of the description. These suggested credits follow the
convention we recommend for citing: title of the item or collection, followed by the collection title, UCD
Digital Library as publisher, and the reference link.
If you are using the item for academic purposes, you may need to follow a particular citation style. UCD
Library has a comprehensive guide for citations and references
(https://libguides.ucd.ie/academicintegrity).

4. Sensitive Material

I have seen something offensive or upsetting in the UCD Digital Library, can it be removed?
Due to the historical nature of the material and the descriptions, there may be content that could cause
distress or offense, or material that would be considered culturally sensitive or inappropriate today.
While UCD Digital Library does not endorse such material, it is made available in the interests of
historical integrity and for research purposes. We aim to indicate potentially offensive or upsetting
content in our descriptive records. If you see something that has not been flagged, please contact us by
email, at digital.library@ucd.ie.

5. Copy-right holders and Take Down Policy

What do I do if I am a rights holder and I am not happy with my items on the Digital Library?
Every effort has been made to trace and contact all copyright holders for content in the UCD Digital
Library. If you are a copyright holder, and you have not been contacted about your content, or we have
not adequately credited you, please contact us at digital.library@ucd.ie.

We have a Take Down Policy for rights holders, which details how we deal with any requests to remove
items from the UCD Digital Library. See the UCD Digital Library Rights, Access, and Re-use Policy for
further information, or contact us directly at digital.library@ucd.ie.

Can I contact you with further information about a copyright holder?
Yes! Contact us directly at digital.library@ucd.ie, detailing the item and collection in question and the
copyright holder information.

internationally recognised set of statements that explain the copyright status of digital objects and how
the objects can be used for different purposes. If you are unclear if your purpose is personal, scholarly,
or commercial, or if you have another use for the item, please contact us at digital.library@ucd.ie for
clarification.

Guide to CC Licenses
Creative Commons licenses are assigned to digital items that are In Copyright, and are a standardised
way for creators to share their work and grant users permission to use their creative works under
copyright law. The Creative Commons license explains what you can do with the work. In general, a work
is given a Creative Commons license in UCD Digital Library when the work is in copyright and UCD have
the permission of the creator to apply the particular license.

There are six different license types, listed from most to least permissive here:

CC BY
This license allows re-users to distribute, remix, adapt, and build upon the material in any medium or
format, so long as attribution is given to the creator. The license allows for commercial use.
    • Share — copy and redistribute the material in any medium or format
    • Adapt — remix, transform, and build upon the material
    • for any purpose, even commercially.
CC BY includes the following elements:
BY – Credit must be given to the creator

CC BY-SA
This license allows re-users to distribute, remix, adapt, and build upon the material in any medium or
format, so long as attribution is given to the creator. The license allows for commercial use. If you remix,
adapt, or build upon the material, you must license the modified material under identical terms.
    • Share — copy and redistribute the material in any medium or format
    • Adapt — remix, transform, and build upon the material
    • for any purpose, even commercially.
CC BY-SA includes the following elements:
BY – Credit must be given to the creator
SA – Adaptations must be shared under the same terms (If you remix, transform, or build upon the
material, you must distribute your contributions under the same license as the original.)

CC BY-NC
This license allows re-users to distribute, remix, adapt, and build upon the material in any medium or
format for non-commercial purposes only, and only so long as attribution is given to the creator.
     • Share — copy and redistribute the material in any medium or format
     • Adapt — remix, transform, and build upon the material
It includes the following elements:
BY – Credit must be given to the creator
NC – Only non-commercial uses of the work are permitted. You may not use the material for commercial
purposes.

Public Domain material

The Creative Commons Public Domain Dedication / CC0
CC0 indicates that the creators have waived their rights under copyright law and put their work into the
worldwide public domain. CC0 allows re-users to copy, adapt, modify, distribute and perform the work
for any purpose, even for commercial purposes, all without asking permission. It is good practice to
credit the creator of the item and to also credit the institution that provided it (like UCD Digital Library)
as it encourages others to make public domain items available online.

The Public Domain Mark (PDM)
The Public Domain Mark (PDM) is applied to digital items which are not protected by copyright or for
which the copyright has expired. Items that are labelled as being in the public domain can be used by
anyone without any restrictions. You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the work, even for
commercial purposes, all without asking permission. It is good practice to credit the creator of the item
and if you also credit the institution that provided it (like UCD Digital Library) as it encourages others to
make public domain items available online.
