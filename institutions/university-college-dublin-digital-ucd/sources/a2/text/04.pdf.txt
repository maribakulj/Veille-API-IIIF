    1. Introduction
The remit of the UCD Digital Library is to maximise the availability of collections of primary source
material for use within an academic and research environment by providing online access to digitised
copies of material held in several UCD repositories, as well as partnered external repositories and
depositors.

The purpose of this policy is to clearly define the conditions under which UCD Digital Library makes all
digital material accessible and re-usable on its public website. This policy covers the legal parameters
under which material is published, including copyright and ethics, and the conditions under which users
can re-use the material, including licensing.

   4. Definitions of Terms Used
   •   Copyright: a form of intellectual property that grants rights to the creators ("authors") of certain
       categories of works, such as books, songs, plays or films. The copyright owner has the right to
       prevent others from reproducing, publishing, performing, communicating to the public or
       adapting their creative work.
   •   Original work: the original, distinct intellectual item, artistic creation, or cultural heritage object.
       An original work can be an analogue object that exists physically such as a book or sculpture, or
       a born digital object that was created in a digital format rather than in a physical form such as a
       digital photograph or a database.
   •   Due diligence: a series of documented enquiries taken to investigate and appraise the copyright
       status of a work, including reasonable efforts to trace the copyright holder and avoid breach of
       copyright.
   •   Archival folders: a folder or file containing multiple individual intellectual items, for example a
       folder containing related letters of correspondence. In UCD Digital Library such archival folders
       are digitised as one digital surrogate to maintain the connection between all the items within
       the folder.
   •   Orphan works: a work which may be in copyright but for which the creator cannot be identified
       and/or the copyright holder cannot be traced, despite reasonable efforts.
   •   Digital surrogate: a copy of an original work made through the process of either digitisation of
       an analogue original or conversion of a born digital original to a format suitable for preservation.
       Also known as a digital object, digital item, or digital content within UCD Digital Library.
   •   Digitisation: the process of converting information from a physical form to a digital one through
       scanning, photography, or other digital capture methods.
   •   Digital preservation: the practice of ensuring digital files are managed and maintained over time
       to ensure that they remain accessible, usable, and understandable through changes in
       technology.
   •   Preservation copy: an uncompressed and uncorrected master digitised file, faithful to the
       original work, and digitised to a very high quality and in a format suitable for secure long term
       digital preservation.
   •   Access copy: a derivative copy created from the preservation file in a format suitable for online
       access or working use rather than long term preservation. An access copy may have been
       processed and may be smaller in size, or lower in quality, than the preservation copy.
   •   Third party copyright: occurs when the rights to copyright works contained within a larger work
       belong to someone other than the creator of the larger work, for example a photograph in a
       book where the author of the book was not the photographer.
   •   Data protection: control of access to and use of personal information that ensures data is kept
       accurate, safe, secure and lawful. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the
       regulation in EU law on data protection and privacy in the European Union (EU).
   •   Metadata: structured descriptive information about the collections and digital material in UCD
       Digital Library. This curated metadata is based on descriptive information, catalogue records, or
       finding aids provided by the depositors/source repositories and is augmented by UCD Digital
       Library professional staff.
   •   Open access: free, unrestricted online access to digital information. The information is open for
       all to search for, read, download, or use within the legal agreements.
   •   Restricted access: control measures placed on digital information for legal or ethical reasons
       such as read only access, access via login, or on-site only access.
   •   Embargo: a period of time during which a user can not access the digital surrogates. Once the
       date for the end of an embargo has passed full access is permitted.
   •   FAIR: a set of guiding principles that aim to improve the Findability, Accessibility,
       Interoperability, and Reuse of digital assets.
   •   Sensitive material: material that has privacy, legal, or ethical concerns. Such material may
       contain personal information, culturally sensitive content, or have the potential to cause distress
       or offence.
   •   Redaction: removing, obscuring, or masking sensitive content due to legal, ethical, or privacy
       concerns.
   •   Public domain: works that are identified as being free from known restrictions under copyright
       law. Copyright and related rights may have expired, been forfeited, expressly waived, or may be
       inapplicable.

5. Policy Declarations
5.1 Copyright
5.1.1 Copyright of Original Works

   •   UCD Digital Library recognises that the creator of the original work, or their estate, is the
       copyright holder, and endeavours to identify the copyright holder where possible.
   •   Due diligence is undertaken to identify and contact copyright holders, with a view to clearing
       copyright.
   •   Where there are multiple copyright statements for a collection of intellectual items contained
       within one digital object, the most restrictive copyright statement is applied to the digital object.
       Users interested in an individual intellectual item should contact the UCD Digital Library to
       ascertain the specific copyright statement. For example, an archival folder containing multiple
       letters from different respondents.

5.1.2 Orphan Works

   •   Orphan Works published in the UCD Digital Library are digitised and made available online under
       the University’s educational and Cultural Heritage preservation remit.
   •   Where the copyright status cannot be ascertained, nor the copyright holders contacted, UCD
       Digital Library undertakes a stringent process to assess the risk involved of publishing the
       surrogate online.
   •   Where a surrogate falls under the EU Orphan Work Directive, a dedicated workflow of
       registering the surrogate in the EUIPO database is undertaken
       (https://euipo.europa.eu/orphanworks/).
    •   Identified orphan works registered in the EUIPO database have their registered number
        recorded in the accompanying descriptive information on the UCD Digital Library. The copyright
        status is updated as further information is received.
    •   UCD Digital Library has a Take Down policy (see 5.4 below) and workflow in place, should
        copyright holders identify their work in the UCD Digital Library, and wish for their works to be
        removed.
    •   UCD Digital Library cannot license for commercial activities any object that is deemed to be an
        orphan work; the onus is on the users to obtain clearance, where possible and at their own cost,
        and to assume the risk, if necessary.

5.1.3 Copyright of Digital Surrogates

    •   Digital surrogates are not considered new works made through the process of digitisation. The
        copyright status of the digital object is the copyright status of the original work. UCD Digital
        Library does not assert copyright ownership over the digital surrogates.
    •   Digitised by us: The creation of 2D digital files via semi-automated digitisation is not protected
        by copyright. Fully human-operated digitisation involving significant creative choices during the
        process, for example 3D scanning or photographic interpretation by professional photographers,
        may be protected by copyright, depending on the process used. UCD Digital Library does not
        assert copyright over any 2D digital files created as part of its digitisation workflows.
    •   Digitised by others: Digital surrogates created by a depositor, or their commissioned vendor, are
        also not copyright protected if created via a semi-automated process. However, they will be
        credited as the creator of the digital asset. Digital surrogates created via a fully human-operated
        process involving significant creative decisions may be protected by copyright, and the copyright
        holder is to be identified and the surrogates licenced for publication by UCD Digital Library.

5.1.4 Copyright of Alternative Versions

    •   Transcription work for UCD Digital Library does not create a new creative work, and as such
        does not incur separate copyright protection. Transcribers, where known, may be publicly
        acknowledged in accordance with GDPR policies.
    •   Translations enjoy the same rights as original works, and as such, are subjected to the same
        rights clearance workflow and acknowledgement.

5.1.5 Third Party Copyright

Third party copyright is cleared on a best effort basis, and in accordance with copyright legislation. It is
noted that photographs and illustrations within larger works are also under the remit of the EU Orphan
Works directive, and are subject to that workflow, if copyright cannot be cleared directly.

5.1.6 Copyright of the UCD Digital Library Descriptive Metadata

All metadata (descriptive information) available on UCD Digital Library are published free of restrictions,
under the terms of the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication. However, if this
metadata is re-used, attribution to UCD Digital Library is encouraged, whenever possible.
5.2 Access Policies
5.2.1 Open Access

    •   UCD Digital Library publishes under the open access principles, where material is as open as
        possible and only restricted when necessary. Collections are accepted on this basis, as per the
        UCD Digital Library Collections Development Policy.
    •   Collections can be accessed for personal and educational use. See section 5.5.2 Commercial Re-
        use conditions for other uses.
    •   UCD Digital Library subscribes to the FAIR data principles.

5.2.2 Access Restrictions

While UCD Digital Library publishes under the open access principles, where material is as open as
possible, on occasion, some access restrictions may be necessary.

5.2.2.1 Permitted Restrictions
Access restrictions may only be applied to collections for copyright, data protection and privacy, and/or
ethical and sensitive material purposes.

5.2.2.2 Embargo of Copyrighted Material
UCD Digital Library works with source repository/depositors to ensure all digital objects are correctly
identified with their copyright and re-use information, and any restricted content is marked with an end
date for such restrictions, should an embargo be required by the creators.

5.2.2.3 Contractual Restrictions
Collections may be restricted due to contractual restrictions by the depositor for a limited period. Such
material will be marked with an end date for the restrictions.

5.3 Sensitive Material Policy

The opinions expressed in the content of UCD Digital Library are those of the individual(s), institution(s)
or corporate body/bodies concerned, and do not necessarily reflect those of the UCD Digital Library or
University College Dublin.

Any material or language considered inappropriate that is not indicated as such in the descriptive
information, should be flagged with the UCD Digital Library by email, at digital.library@ucd.ie.

5.3.1 Culturally sensitive or offensive content

UCD Digital Library collects material from various sources to preserve and give access to the historical
record. Much of this material is historical in nature and reflects the particular time, place, and society in
which it was produced or collected. There may be material that could cause distress or offense, or
material that would be considered culturally sensitive or inappropriate today. While UCD Digital Library
does not endorse such material, it is made available, in its original form and context, in the interests of
historical integrity and for research purposes.
UCD Digital Library aims to provide contextual information for such material through descriptive records
and, where necessary, through statements on individual collections or items advising about particularly
sensitive content.

5.3.2 Language in descriptions

UCD Digital Library aims to describe material accurately, respectfully, and in a way that will not cause
distress or offense. Descriptions in UCD Digital Library are often based on descriptions, finding aids, or
catalogue records from depositors. Such descriptions may have been created many years ago and may
contain language that would be considered inappropriate today. UCD Digital Library strives to revise and
update such language where possible. It may occasionally be necessary to retain language from
historical material in the descriptions for historical accuracy or context. In such cases the UCD Digital
Library will try to clearly indicate such language with quotation marks, notes, or other explanation.

5.3.3 Redaction of material

UCD Digital Library aims to provide material in as close to its original form and context as possible, even
if this means retaining content or language that may be considered inappropriate today. Redaction of
material is only done when deemed absolutely necessary due to legal, data protection, or ethical
requirements. Redaction is always done on the Access copies of the digital material only, with the
original content retained in full in the Preservation copy.

5.4 UCD Digital Library Take Down Policy

On receipt of a complaint relating to a potential copyright infringement, violation of moral rights,
violation of data protection, or the discovery of unflagged sensitive content, a member of UCD Digital
Library staff will:

    •   Acknowledge the complaint, in writing usually within 30 calendar days of receiving it.
    •   Make an initial judgment (obtaining advice from a suitable individual of university staff if
        necessary) of the validity of the complaint and:
             o if the complaint is plausible based on Irish law, the resource will be removed, or access
                 will be suspended pending verification of the complaint.
             o if the complaint is judged to be implausible or incorrect, we will inform the complainant
                 of this and our reasons.
    •   Where necessary, staff will seek professional legal advice on the complaint.
    •   UCD Digital Library will advise the person who deposited the resource in question, outline the
        nature of the complaint and give them the opportunity to refute the complaint.
    •   UCD Digital Library will investigate the complaint in full, obtaining advice as required, and
        contact the complainant and the depositor with the result of this investigation and what action
        we have taken/will take.
    •   If the investigation finds in the complainant's favour, then UCD Digital Library will remove the
        resource permanently from the repository unless the complainant gives us permission to retain
        it. The records of an item will only be withdrawn from view for reasons such as their being found
        to violate the legal rights of any person.

Please contact the UCD Digital Library at digital.library@ucd.ie with any complaints.
5.5 Re-use and Licensing Policies
5.5.1 General Re-use

All material made available in UCD Digital Library is primarily for educational and personal use, as per
the agreements with known rights holders and the owners of the physical collections. The copyright
status of all material is reflected in the online descriptive information as a rights statement, with an
accompanying license, where applicable. The onus is on the user to ensure that they abide by the
licensing and copyright requirements, as described in this online description of the material.

5.5.2 Commercial Re-use

5.5.2.1 Public Domain Material
Public domain material is free to use for some commercial activities (see Appendix: Current Standards
Used to Represent Rights in UCD Digital Library), as per the prior agreement of the owners of the
material. Some contractual restrictions may apply to this material and will be recorded in the descriptive
information.

5.5.2.2 Material in Copyright
Copyrighted material can only be re-used by agreement with the copyright holders and/or owners of the
physical collection, unless otherwise stated in the rights information, for example, where no additional
rights or contractual agreements have been applied; or objects that have been assigned a Creative
Commons license that allows for commercial re-use by the copyright holder.

5.5.2.3 Orphan Works
Material identified as orphan works are made available on the UCD Digital Library only for personal and
educational use and are marked for re-use for non-commercial purposes only.

Users wishing to re-use this material for commercial purposes must undertake their own copyright
clearance endeavours, as these cannot be cleared by the UCD Digital Library. UCD Digital Library cannot
license for commercial activities any object that is deemed to be an orphan work; the onus is on the
users to obtain clearance, where possible and at their own cost, and to assume the risk, if necessary.

5.5.2.4 Copyright Holder Identification
UCD Digital Library endeavours, where appropriate under the General Data Protection Regulation
(GDPR), to provide users with the information to facilitate the clearance of copyright material for
commercial use. Where a user identifies the copyright holder of an orphan work, please contact the UCD
Digital Library so that the descriptive information can be updated.

5.5.3 Image Supply

All images on the UCD Digital Library are available for download and will, in general, suit most purposes.
UCD Digital Library can only supply images that have already been digitised and published on our
website and cannot undertake digitisation of other material. If users require a copy of the images at
higher quality for specific purposes, contact UCD Digital Library in the first instance. There may be delays
in the supply of the images, due to workload and staffing issues. Please allow 10 working days for the
supply of the images. Some administrative charges may apply.
Copyrighted images being used for commercial purposes must have been cleared by the copyright
holder, before a high-resolution image is supplied by UCD Digital Library. For orphan works, UCD Digital
Library will not supply a high-resolution copy unless proof of clearance and the resulting license for re-
use is furnished by the user.

5.6 GDPR Policy

UCD Digital Library adheres to the Data Protection Act 2018, and complies with internal University
College Dublin policies around the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), found online at:
http://www.ucd.ie/gdpr/policiesprocedures/.

5.6.1 Collected Data

As most data residing within the UCD Digital Library is made available on an Open Access basis, users are
not generally asked to create an account or login.

With the small number of resources to which access is restricted, users are asked to complete a
registration form that requests access to the restricted resources, which they send directly to the parent
repository for processing. Upon approval, users’ basic details are added to the UCD Digital Library Access
Control Lists. User details are deleted one year after the last login by the user and are only required for
authentication purposes.

5.6.2 Usage Statistics

UCD Digital Library utilises anonymised user analytics to track usage of the digital collections for
statistical purposes. Data collected includes geographic regions, internet domain, language, pages
viewed and for how long. This information is used to report back to parent repositories on their
individual collections, and to help UCD Digital Library to improve the user experience.

5.6.3 Cookies

UCD Digital Library utilises cookies to help users to navigate efficiently and to perform certain functions,
and to track how users use the website. The cookies do not return any personal information about the
user to UCD Digital Library and are stored locally on the user’s computer. UCD Digital Library aligns with
the UCD Cookie Policy, found here: http://www.ucd.ie/cookie-policy/.

5.6.4 User Email Queries

Any information users provide in an email to the UCD Digital Library email account is not made available
to any third parties, other than those working with the UCD Digital Library on normal University
business. It is used by the UCD Digital Library only in accordance with the purpose for which the user
provided the information.

General queries received via emails will receive replies, and the conversation will only be retained for
that calendar year. The email query type may be recorded in a general statistics log. No personal details
or the specific nature of the enquiry will be recorded.
Copyright and re-use emails will have a longer retention period, depending on the nature of the email.
   • Emails requesting permission to re-use digital files will be retained for a period of 5 years.
   • Emails granting permission for re-use of digital files will be retained indefinitely.

The email query type may be recorded in a general statistics log. Personal details and the specific nature
of the permission granted may be recorded.

     1. Rights Statements
UCD Digital Library utilises the rightsstatements.org framework for the identification of the copyright
status of material (Works) published in its open access platform.

1.1 Rights Statements for material in Copyright

The following rights statements are intended for use with digital objects that are in copyright.

IN COPYRIGHT
This Rights Statement is used for a digital surrogate that is in copyright. The use of this statement
implies that the rights-holder has granted UCD Digital Library permission to make the Work available or
that the Work is made available under an exception or limitation to copyright.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC/1.0/

IN COPYRIGHT - EU ORPHAN WORK
This Rights Statement is used for Works that have been identified as Orphan Works in accordance with
Directive 2012/28/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2012 on certain
permitted uses of Orphan Works. For this digital surrogate, either (a) no rights-holder(s) have been
identified or (b) one or more rights-holder(s) have been identified but none have been located even
though a diligent search for the rights-holder(s) has been conducted. The Work will be registered in the
EU Orphan Works Database maintained by EUIPO.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC-OW-EU/1.0/

IN COPYRIGHT - EDUCATIONAL USE PERMITTED
This Rights Statement can be used only for copyrighted Works for which the rights-holder(s) have
explicitly authorized its use by third parties for educational purposes without first obtaining permission.
For other uses, contact the rights-holder(s).
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC-EDU/1.0/

IN COPYRIGHT - NON-COMMERCIAL USE PERMITTED
This Rights Statement is used for copyrighted Works which have been explicitly authorized by the rights-
holder(s) to allow third parties to use the Work for non-commercial purposes without obtaining
permission first. For other uses contact the rights-holder(s).
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC-NC/1.0/

IN COPYRIGHT - RIGHTS-HOLDER(S) UNLOCATABLE OR UNIDENTIFIABLE
This Rights Statement is used for a Work that has been identified as in copyright but for which no rights-
holder(s) has been identified or located after some reasonable investigation. This statement is used for
works not covered by the EU Orphan Works Directive.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC-RUU/1.0/

1.2 Rights statements for objects that are not in copyright

The following rights statements are intended for works that are not in copyright but where there are
restrictions other than copyright that prevent free re-use or where the out of copyright status has only
been ascertained for a specific jurisdiction. These rights statements should only be used when it is not
possible to use the Public Domain Mark or the CC0 Public Domain Dedication.

NO COPYRIGHT - CONTRACTUAL RESTRICTIONS
This Rights Statement is used for Works that are in the Public Domain but for which the data provider
has entered into a contractual agreement that requires it to take steps to restrict third party uses of the
Work. In order for this Rights Statement to be conclusive, the data provider must provide a link to a
page detailing the contractual restrictions (such as privacy issues, cultural protections, digitization
agreements or donor agreements) that apply to the use of the Item.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-CR/1.0/

NO COPYRIGHT - NON-COMMERCIAL USE ONLY
This Rights Statement is used for Works that are in the Public Domain and have been digitized in a
public-private partnership as part of which, the partners have agreed to limit commercial uses of this
digital representation of the Work by third parties.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-NC/1.0/

NO COPYRIGHT - OTHER KNOWN LEGAL RESTRICTIONS
This Rights Statement is used for Items that are in the public domain but that cannot be freely re-used
as the consequence of known legal restrictions that prevent the data provider from allowing free re-use
of the Work, such as cultural heritage or traditional cultural expression protections. In order for this
Rights Statement to be conclusive, the data provider must provide a link to a page detailing the legal
restrictions that limit re-use of the Work.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-OKLR/1.0/

1.3 Other rights statements

The following rights statements are intended for use with digital objects where the copyright status has
not been determined with certainty. These should only be used if it is not possible to use a clearer rights
statement or license.

COPYRIGHT UNDETERMINED
This Rights Statement is used for Works for which the copyright status is unknown and for which the
organization that has made the Work available has undertaken an effort to determine the copyright
status of the Work. Typically, this Rights Statement is used when the organization is missing key facts
essential to making an accurate copyright status determination.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/UND/1.0/

NO KNOWN COPYRIGHT
This Rights Statement is used for Works for which the copyright status has not been determined
conclusively, but for which the data provider has reasonable cause to believe that the work is not
covered by copyright or related rights anymore.
URI: http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NKC/1.0/

COPYRIGHT NOT EVALUATED
This Rights Statement is not being implemented in UCD Digital Library, as all works published in UCD
Digital Library are assessed for copyright.

    2. Creative Commons Licenses
Re-use licenses, using the Creative Commons framework, are assigned by the copyright holders only.
UCD Digital Library encourages copyright holders to choose the most open licence possible.

The following licenses are utilised in the UCD Digital Library:

Public Domain Mark (No known copyright)
This work has been identified as being free of known restrictions under copyright law, including all
related and neighbouring rights.

3. Other Rights Metadata
3.1 Embedded Metadata in Images

UCD Digital Library adds ownership and contact information about digital collections to the image
headers, using the IPTC (International Press Telecommunications Council) Photo Metadata standard
(https://iptc.org/standards/photo-metadata/).

3.2 System Rights Files

UCD Digital Library also employs the use of an internal Rights Metadata standard which contains rights
information in machine and human readable form. The file contains generic information about
Copyright (also contained in more detail in the descriptive metadata), Use (also contained in the
descriptive metadata), and Access (which is required as part of the Access Restrictions infrastructure of
the UCD Digital Library).
